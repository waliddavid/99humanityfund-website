# Data: Phased Implementation (Year-by-Year Rollout)

This is the year-by-year rollout file behind the Part 6 timeline. It traces each country's manufacturing employment across a baseline year and five five-year tranches under two scenarios: **Informal Insertion** (the proposed implementation) and **Balanced** (what fuller fairness would require). For each scenario it also gives the per-tranche change (T1 through T5) and the total baseline-to-end change.

The dates are illustrative. Because the fund's own founding phase comes first, the manufacturing relocation begins at The Launch — so the rollout maps to roughly 2032 (start of relocation planning) through 2061 (end of Tranche 5), with the relocation planning phase running 2032–2036 and the five tranches following through 2061. A note on the column labels below: the downloadable data file's year-columns are 2032, 2036, 2041, 2046, 2051, 2056, and 2061 — the start of relocation planning, the end of planning (the 2036 baseline snapshot, just before Tranche 1), and the five tranche-end milestones.

All employment figures are in **thousands of jobs**. So China's baseline figure of roughly 215,000 means about 215 million workers. The planning phase (the first two year-columns) shows no employment changes — that's why the first two columns are identical for every country, since Tranche 1 only begins after planning.

## What the columns mean

The downloadable CSV has 30 columns: identity fields plus two parallel scenario blocks (Informal Insertion and Balanced), each with seven milestone-year values, five per-tranche deltas, and a total delta.

<p class="note-line">Coverage: 194 countries. The relocation planning phase (2032–2036, which follows the fund's own 2027–2031 founding phase) has no employment changes, so the 2032 and 2036 values are identical. Full methodology, tranche sequencing, and scenario definitions are in [Article 6-2 — The Redistribution Methodology](/part-6/6-2-the-redistribution-methodology/). Data sources include UN WPP 2024, ILO ILOSTAT, UNIDO INDSTAT, UN Comtrade, World Bank WGI/WDI/LPI, UNESCO UIS, USGS, and the Energy Institute — all consulted May 2026.</p>
